prototype 1: Disasters can take place any time. It's important to be prepared for, properly respond to, and recover from said disasters. The problem is disaster information is spread across many repositories , fragmented known as silos. These data silos often compliment each other or further elaborate on each other but alone they aren't as useful. The emergency responders, disaster scientist, and the general public affected by these disasters may not have the time to consolidate all these fragmented information and the general public may not have the means to these resources locked behind well funded agencies, large cities,  elite research universities or understand the jargon. The aim of this project is to democratize technology by providing a plain language query interface with real-time accurate information

prototype 2: Disaster related info is scattered across many archives, which makes it harder to have complete understanding of the topic because some works complement each other or further elaborates on it, and many people affected may not have the time to consolidate it all.
These archives are needed for decision making, long term or quick, by common people affected by these disasters, disaster science researchers who study these events and write ontologies about them, and first responders or emergency managers who act based on this info
This information might also be hard to interpret due to the jargon, technical or software skills needed to extract the info, or lack of general access to these things.
